🏠🏢 Residential vs. Commercial Rehab Checklist
Step	Residential Rehab 🏠	Commercial Rehab 🏢
1. Initial Walkthrough & Documentation	Inspect structure, systems, roof, finishes. Take photos, notes, measurements.	Same as residential, but also check ADA compliance, fire suppression, egress, and zoning use.
2. Define Scope of Work	Cosmetic vs. gut rehab, layout changes, add bathrooms, kitchens, etc.	May involve repurposing (e.g. office to restaurant), compliance upgrades, new tenant layouts.
3. Engage Professionals	Architect (if major changes), GC or subs, maybe structural engineer.	Architect often required. Engage MEP engineers, code consultant, structural engineer.
4. Obtain As-Builts / Create Drawings	Create drawings only if layout changes or permits required.	As-builts often needed. Full stamped drawings usually required.
5. Permitting	Apply for permits based on SOW: demo, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, structural.	More permits often required: use change, occupancy, fire/life safety, ADA. Requires stamped plans.
6. Hazard Testing	Lead, asbestos, mold if pre-1980s. Remediate as required by law.	Required in almost all older buildings. Follow OSHA and EPA standards for mitigation.
7. Demo & Site Prep	Selective or full interior gut. May not need heavy equipment.	Often requires coordination with structural team, temp shoring, larger demo crews.
8. Rough Construction & Systems	Foundation repair, framing, MEP rough-ins, insulation.	Larger HVAC, plumbing systems. Fire-rated assemblies, data/telecom wiring, backup power.
9. Inspections (Rough & Final)	Schedule rough and final for each permitted trade.	Staged inspections. Fire marshal and life safety inspections often required.
10. Finish Work	Drywall, flooring, cabinetry, painting, fixtures.	Same, but includes ADA-compliant finishes, commercial-grade materials.
11. Punch List & Corrections	Final walkthrough with owner. Fix minor items.	Detailed owner punch list. May involve multiple stakeholders or tenant reps.
12. Certificate of Occupancy or Final Approval	Required if structural work, addition, or full gut. Not always for cosmetic-only jobs.	Always required before legal occupancy. Includes fire/life safety, ADA, parking, and signage compliance.
13. Handover & Closeout	Provide manuals, warranties, photos, permits.	O&M manuals, as-builts, fire inspection records, elevator certs, compliance docs.

📌 Notes:
ADA Compliance: Required for commercial properties—includes entrances, bathrooms, counters, etc.

Change of Use: Commercial projects often trigger change-of-use reviews, which can involve additional codes and upgrades.

Financing: Commercial rehabs often require progress draws, inspections, and bank approvals throughout the project.

Occupancy Load: Commercial properties are subject to capacity limits and egress standards based on use type.
